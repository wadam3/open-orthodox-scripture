Brenton is **not our Greek text**, is **not** used to generate our wording, and does not constitute Orthodox ecclesiastical review. We record conceptual differences instead of importing his English:
- **1:2:** His old English emphasizes appearance and lack of furnishing; our own “unseen and unformed” follows Greek `ἀόρατος` and `ἀκατασκεύαστος` and is provisionally preferred for audio.
- **1:5:** His edition uses “first day”; our “one day” deliberately follows Greek `ἡμέρα μία`. Reader translation note `GEN-001-TRN-005`.
- **1:6–8:** Historical “firmament” versus our “expanse” reflects an English term choice for `στερέωμα`. **Both** the old English and the **1887 Greek** include the second-day goodness statement; preserve it. No Hebrew comparison is part of this audit.
- **1:20–21:** Brenton uses a narrower older zoological English label for water creatures and for the `κήτη`; our revised 1:20 “living creatures that move” and 1:21 “great sea creatures” aim to avoid misleading modern categories. These are Greek-driven decisions, **not revisions copied from Brenton**.
- **1:26–27:** “Humankind” versus old “man” is an inclusive singular-collective English handling of Greek `ἄνθρωπον`; Greek singular/plural pronouns remain visible in our rendering.
- **1:30–31:** Our idiom “has life within it” paraphrases `ψυχὴν ζωῆς` and remains open for refinement. In 1:31 “behold” reflects Greek `ἰδού`. Neither choice depends on copying historical English.

## 1887 first-edition printed bracket and manuscript findings

### GEN-001-SRC-001 — why the first chapter is not from the surviving Vaticanus opening

As documented in the book/source history, the surviving Codex Vaticanus lacks the beginning of Genesis. **Swete prints Genesis 1** primarily from the Codex Alexandrinus tradition where Vaticanus is incomplete. The manuscript gap is not a missing Swete chapter. We directly observed the edition's **A** marginal witness identifier on the 1887 printed pages. Reader source note `GEN-001-SRC-001` is retained.

### GEN-001-ED-008 — verified 1887 verse 8 wording

The **printed p. 1** image clearly contains the Greek wording `καὶ εἶδεν ὁ θεὸς ὅτι καλόν` after naming the expanse Heaven. Keep the statement in our English. **Do not frame this as a Hebrew comparison or a verified dispute among other Greek manuscripts.** The exact quoted Greek spelling here is a short reading from the historic printed image; the full chapter still awaits independent diplomatic transcription.

### GEN-001-SRC-002 — bracketed gaps in Alexandrinus, printed p. 2, Genesis 1:20–25

Swete's **original 1887 printed p. 2** has the apparatus notice **“20–25 quae uncis incl sunt perier in A”**: the words enclosed in brackets within the indicated verses have perished in manuscript **A** (Alexandrinus). This confirms that the **brackets are present in our selected first edition**, not merely reported in later 1901 digital texts. They mark reconstructed/supplemented printed readings where Alexandrinus is defective; the original Greek edition includes the supplied wording, but not all such words survive in Alexandrinus. Do not assert without further evidence which other Greek witness supplied every specific word, and do not claim that the entire verses are missing; the notice concerns the **bracketed portions**.

**Reader-facing explanation:** `GEN-001-SRC-002`, applied to 1:20–25, with a link to this audit. The source-side question is partly resolved (actual 1887 bracket marks and their basic meaning verified), while exact bracket coordinates, alternative witness support and word-level reconstruction remain open.

### GEN-001-SRC-003 — another Alexandrinus gap starts at 1:29, printed p. 3

Swete's **original 1887 printed p. 3** says **“29–II 3 quae uncis incl sunt perier in A”**: bracketed passages lost from Alexandrinus extend from **Genesis 1:29 through Genesis 2:3**. This overlaps our chapter at 1:29–31. As above, this concerns the **bracketed words, not every word of the entire verse range**. We include the 1887 edited passage in the provisional English but do not attribute uncertain reconstructed words directly to surviving A manuscript leaves.

**Reader-facing explanation:** `GEN-001-SRC-003`, applied to 1:29–31, with a carry-forward flag for **Genesis 2:1–3**. The 1887 bracket's broad range and meaning have now been verified, while detailed restored-word witness attribution and chapter 2 continuation require research.

### Source-wide decision status

These findings **partly resolve** the original generic bracket warning: we have now inspected **the chosen 1887 first-edition** printed images and verified the original edition's actual notes. The earlier warning against attributing a *later* Swete electronic transcription to the first edition **remains valid**, but it is no longer accurate to call **all bracket reporting** unverified. The editorial treatment of the specific supplied readings, and a complete independently checked diplomatic Greek source transcription, remain unfinished. Keep `SWETE-FLAG-002` open for those remaining elements.

## Second focused 1887 image pass: verse 14 — added governing clause

The first printing **visibly includes** `καὶ ἄρχειν τῆς ἡμέρας καὶ τῆς νυκτὸς` as part of Genesis 1:14, crossing from printed page 1 into the first line of page 2. The provisional English **previously omitted this clause**. The actual image is [Swete 1887 printed page 2](https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/lxx1/lxx1/png/0030=2.png); [page 1](https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/lxx1/lxx1/png/0029=1.png) begins 1:14. **Correction applied:** `to govern the day and the night` has been inserted in verse 14 alongside the day/night separation wording, and a reader-facing note `GEN-001-TRN-014` has been attached. This is **not** a doctrinal reinterpretation or Hebrew-based harmonization; it repairs a missing Greek clause in our first draft. Because some other Greek presentations lack the phrase, this first-edition choice requires a future proper source-variant/witness collation, not a silent change of base text.
