# Genesis 2 — first-pass Greek-oriented translation and textual research

**Research date:** 2026-11-12 (US Eastern). **Status:** Provisional first-pass study, **not** independently source-verified, Orthodox-reviewed, or publication-approved. The companion [25-verse English pilot](../../../translation/ot/GEN/002.json) is a newly prepared translation draft, not a reproduction of any copyrighted modern English edition. The source to control later checks is H. B. Swete, *The Old Testament in Greek According to the Septuagint*, vol. I, **Cambridge 1887 first edition**.

## Photographic printed sources and boundaries

- [1887 printed p. 3, scanned image](https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/lxx1/lxx1/png/0031=3.png), [page viewer](https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/lxx1/lxx1/Page_3.html): includes the close of Genesis 1 and **Genesis 2:1–16**, including the start of 2:16 near the bottom.
- [1887 printed p. 4, scanned image](https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/lxx1/lxx1/png/0032=4.png), [page viewer](https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/lxx1/lxx1/Page_4.html): continues **Genesis 2:17–25**, followed by Genesis 3. The opening 2:17 prints the continuation of Adam's command from 2:16.
- [Internet Archive edition record](https://archive.org/details/oldtestamentingr01swet); [Project source decision](../../../docs/SEPTUAGINT_SOURCE_DECISION.md). We have not imported or independently certified a verse-aligned Greek diplomatic text from these images.

The old page **3** note reads `29—II 3 quae uncis incl sunt perier in A`: the portions enclosed by editorial brackets from Genesis **1:29 through 2:3** are lost from surviving Codex Alexandrinus (**A**). **This does not mean whole verses are absent from every ancient witness**, nor that Swete's editorial brackets are narration punctuation. Exact letter/diacritic/bracket boundaries require character-by-character and second-review work. This chapter carries forward the unresolved Genesis 1 bracket-map work, not a completed verification.

## First-pass translation decisions, all provisional

| Verse | Original 1887 Greek reading or issue | English choice and unresolved question |
|---|---|---|
| 2:1 | `καὶ συνετελέσθησαν`, with `ὁ οὐρανὸς καὶ` bracketed in the photograph | Draft begins **“And heaven and earth were completed”**. Removed an earlier unsupported “In the end.” The rendered “ordered array” for `κόσμος` is a provisional lexical choice. |
| 2:2 | `τῇ ἕκτῃ` (“on the **sixth**”), followed by resting on the `ἑβδόμῃ` (“seventh”) | **Preserve the 1887 Greek's sixth-day completion**, rather than silently conforming to a different traditional textual line. Exact square-bracket boundaries around supplied material remain open. |
| 2:3 | Restoration brackets are still in the 1:29–2:3 manuscript-loss span | English reads the complete edited line. Diplomatic transcription must mark what is reconstructed, while smooth narration can read it uninterrupted with accessible notes. |
| 2:4 | `Αὕτη ἡ βίβλος γενέσεως` | “This is the book of the origin”; check whether “account of the origin” is more natural for final narration without losing the Greek “book.” |
| 2:6 | `πηγὴ δὲ ἀνέβαινεν` | “A spring rose,” not an assumed Hebrew-source “mist”; check whether “fountain” better suits the Greek and narration. |
| 2:7 | `πνοὴν ζωῆς`, `ψυχὴν ζῶσαν` | “Breath of life” and “living soul.” Retain semantic distinction; review “into his face” versus “into his nostrils.” |
| 2:8–9 | `παράδεισον ἐν Ἐδὲμ`, `ξύλον τῆς ζωῆς`, `ξύλον ... γινώσκειν καλὸν καὶ πονηρόν` | “Paradise in Eden,” “tree of life,” and “tree of knowing good and evil”; confirm whether the latter sounds natural while tracking the Greek infinitive. |
| 2:10–14 | `Φεισών`, `Εὐιλάτ`, `Γηών`, `Αἰθιοπίας`, `Τίγρις`, `Εὐφράτης` | Keep Greek-based river/place names provisionally; choose stable pronunciation/spelling guide before audio. |
| 2:12 | `ἄνθραξ`, `ὁ λίθος ὁ πράσινος` | “Carbuncle” and “green stone” are historical Greek-based working labels, not identified modern minerals; require lexical/source review. |
| 2:15 | `ἐργάζεσθαι`, `φυλάσσειν` | “Work it and guard it.” Check “tend”/“keep” for final narration. |
| 2:16–17 | Command crosses the page-3/4 boundary. Verse 17 visibly uses plural `φάγεσθε` and `φάγητε` | English “you” is number-neutral; record **Greek plural forms** in verse 17 and decide whether and how to explain them. The warning's dying formula is preserved as “you shall surely die.” |
| 2:18–20 | `ποιήσωμεν` and `βοηθὸν κατ᾽ αὐτόν`, `ὅμοιος αὐτῷ` | “Let us make” is **Greek plural**, while “helper corresponding to him” is provisional. Avoid an ungrounded reading from other languages. |
| 2:21–22 | `μίαν τῶν πλευρῶν`, `ᾠκοδόμησεν` | “One of his ribs” and “fashioned”; “side” and “built” are alternatives to be considered with Greek lexical evidence and the chapter's tone. |
| 2:23–25 | `Γυνή`, `ἀνδρός`; `οἱ δύο γυμνοί` | Retain “Woman” and “man,” and the two naked without shame. |

## Reader-facing theological/textual notes, not endorsements

The source line at 2:2 explicitly says God completed work **on the sixth day** and rested **on the seventh**. The source cannot be presented as if it says “completed on the seventh” merely because some familiar English editions do. In 2:18, the first-person plural `ποιήσωμεν` must not be flattened to a singular. These are **Greek-text decisions**, not independent doctrinal rulings or a claim of endorsement from the Eastern Orthodox Church.
