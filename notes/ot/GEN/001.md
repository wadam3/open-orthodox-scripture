## Reader-facing manuscript-context note

**GEN-001-SRC-001 — applies to the chapter introduction, especially 1:1–5.**

> The surviving ancient Codex Vaticanus does not preserve the opening chapters of Genesis. For those chapters Swete's printed Greek Septuagint uses other early manuscript evidence, especially Codex Alexandrinus. This is a gap in a surviving manuscript, not an omission of Genesis 1 from Swete's edition.

This is a **manuscript-provenance note**, not a claim that a different wording has been identified in Genesis 1:1–5. No passage-specific variant or theological difference is marked verified merely because Vaticanus is missing. Link the source note in print/ebook and provide an accessible source-note transcript or optional audio segment; the primary narration should remain uninterrupted.

## First independent English style samples

These are **our original provisional English options**, produced prior to any passage-by-passage use of Brenton. The structured chapter draft uses the clearer audio-oriented wording as a working sample, **not a permanent style decision**. The traditional alternative is for listening comparison.

| Verse | Traditional / literary possibility | Clear spoken-English working possibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | In the beginning God made the heaven and the earth. | **In the beginning God made heaven and earth.** |
| 2 | Now the earth was unseen and unformed; darkness was over the deep, and the Spirit of God moved over the water. | **The earth was unseen and unformed, and darkness lay over the deep. And the Spirit of God was moving over the water.** |
| 3 | And God said, “Let light come to be”; and light came to be. | **And God said, “Let there be light.” And there was light.** |
| 4 | And God saw that the light was good; and God divided the light from the darkness. | **God saw that the light was good, and God separated the light from the darkness.** |
| 5 | And God named the light Day, and the darkness he named Night. Evening came, and morning came: one day. | **God called the light Day, and the darkness he called Night. And evening came, and morning came: one day.** |

**Translation questions to resolve after original Greek transcription is fully audited:**

- **GEN-001-TC-001 (1:1):** `ἐποίησεν` can be rendered “made”; “created” may convey the context but is not an identical English verb. Greek `οὐρανόν` is grammatically singular; decide “heaven” versus idiomatic “heavens” from a documented register rule.
- **GEN-001-TC-002 (1:2):** `ἀόρατος` and `ἀκατασκεύαστος` favor “unseen” and “unformed/unprepared” over silently importing English from another published Bible. `πνεῦμα θεοῦ` can pose a Spirit/wind interpretation question; select an English reading from Greek syntax and Orthodox usage after audit, without claiming it is a confirmed Greek manuscript difference.
- **GEN-001-TC-003 (1:3):** `γενηθήτω` / `ἐγένετο` share a “come to be” sense; “let there be / there was” may sound more natural but loses the repeated verb family. Compare aloud.
- **GEN-001-TC-004 (1:4):** Repetition of “God” is deliberate and may support a solemn cadence; compare against a pronoun to avoid distracting repetition.
- **GEN-001-TC-005 (1:5):** `ἡμέρα μία` is literally “one day”; do not silently change it to “the first day” without an editorial decision. “Evening came, and morning came” preserves the repeated rhythm.

These are semantic discussion prompts, **not independently verified diplomatic quotations from the 1887 page**; Greek diacritics and precise punctuation in any Greek snippets require the historical page re-audit.
