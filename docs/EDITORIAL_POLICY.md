# Editorial standards — provisional draft

## Aim and independence

Translate faithfully from documented Greek source editions into readable English while respecting Eastern Orthodox scriptural and theological tradition. This is an **independent project**, not affiliated with, sponsored by, endorsed by, or approved by an Orthodox Church, jurisdiction, diocese, or ecclesiastical authority. An individual review does not imply institution-wide approval.

## Source and canon policy

The first pilot is **Genesis chapter 1** from a specifically identified, rights-reviewed **Greek Septuagint** edition. Define the precise Greek edition and its revision/verse numbering *before* importing its text. The antiquity of the underlying Greek scriptures does not imply that modern edited editions, digitizations, translations, apparatus, or annotations can be copied freely.

Record the full Orthodox Old Testament inventory and book order, with documented jurisdictional or textual variations. After the provisional Old Testament draft, choose a precise rights-reviewed Greek New Testament edition and document its relationship to Orthodox textual traditions.

## Independent sources, discrepancies, and publication notes

- **No third-party permission or licensing requests for source texts.** Base the project on verified public-domain textual material and independently created translation and notes. Do not import a modern edition, licensed transcription, commentary, apparatus, or audio performance when redistribution depends on third-party permission or restrictive conditions.
- Record full identifying information and the public-domain basis for every actual source in the internal source register. Publishing generic descriptions does **not** remove the need to prove provenance or comply with rights; do not erase historically necessary references.
- Whenever a meaningful variation is found in Greek witnesses, book arrangement, verse numbering, or documented Orthodox usage, create a separate book-specific research entry **and a linked footnote/explainer attached to the affected passage**. Explain the difference neutrally and note unresolved questions.
- Reader-facing explanatory notes should avoid proprietary product/publisher names and copyrighted quotations. Use original language and references to independently reusable ancient witnesses. If evidence is only found in a restricted source, flag the issue for further research rather than copying or claiming to have verified it.
- Carry substantive explanations into print and ebooks, with accessible accompanying notes for audio. No source comparison implies official Church approval.

## Historical English translation checks

After each original Greek-based English draft and independent internal Greek audit, the project's [Brenton reference policy](HISTORICAL_ENGLISH_TRANSLATION_REFERENCES.md) permits comparison with a verified nineteenth-century public-domain English Septuagint printing. Brenton's translation is **not** the Greek input and must not be copied or modernized to generate our original translation. Record meaningful differences, Greek evidence, and decision reasons in book notes, and use the usual variant/interpretation footnote requirements. Verify the exact printing and its coverage; do not import modern electronic transcriptions without an independently adequate public-domain/provenance basis.

## Translation conventions to decide

- Style/register: whether to prefer traditional/liturgical diction or contemporary readable English. **Open decision**; compare short sample passages before finalizing.
- Names/transliterations, capitalization, the divine name, verse numbering, punctuation, poetic formatting, and recurring theological vocabulary.
- How to document variants, translator clarifications, cross-references, difficult syntax, and interpretive disputes.
- A stable glossary and reproducible chapter/verse metadata.

## Initial, pre-collaborator translation process

An internal AI-assisted process generates and checks **provisional** English drafts chapter by chapter. Document exact sources, English options considered, AI involvement, potential omissions/mistranslations, variants, and outstanding questions. An independent internal AI comparison is a useful quality check but is **not** Greek-language or Orthodox theological validation.

**No outside contributors or qualified review group will be recruited until the entire initial Bible draft is complete**, per the project's chosen sequence. Early public files are working drafts, not reviewed releases.

## Post-draft human approval gates

When the **full provisional Bible draft** exists, invite qualified Greek and English editors and qualified Orthodox scholars or clergy to review it. For each chapter prior to approved publication:

1. Confirm source-edition rights and provenance.
2. Obtain language-editor confirmation of Greek/English accuracy.
3. Obtain named qualified Orthodox-reviewer sign-off for intended theological/liturgical suitability, with permission to publish reviewers' names.
4. Confirm final text, variant notes, acknowledgments, publication version, and open-licensing attribution via release editor.

GitHub merge rights, contributor counts, and AI confidence are not ecclesiastical authority. Do not claim the project is Church-approved without actual Church authorization.
