# Editorial standards (draft)

## Aim

Favor fidelity to the Greek source, clarity in contemporary English, transparent treatment of textual variants, and consistency with Orthodox theology and liturgical terminology. Avoid claims of being the Church's official translation without explicit authorization.

## Source selection

Choose a precisely identified Greek New Testament edition after assessing textual tradition, scholarly quality, provenance, permission to reproduce, and suitability for Orthodox readers. The biblical work's antiquity does not automatically make a modern edited transcription, translation, annotation, or digital compilation reusable.

For a later Old Testament phase, center the Septuagint tradition and consult qualified Orthodox reviewers on edition, book inclusion/order, and translation conventions. Do not assume that all Orthodox jurisdictions use identical published lists or spellings.

## Chapter-level requirements

Each chapter records its book, chapter, edition provenance, status, ordered verses, and reviewer fields. For difficult passages, document literal glosses, alternate readings, theological stakes, and final reasons. Do not silently replace the base text with a modern English translation.

## Review gates

1. Provenance and rights confirmed.
2. Language editor signs off on Greek and English accuracy.
3. Named qualified Orthodox reviewer signs off on theological and liturgical suitability.
4. Release editor verifies version metadata and unresolved notes.

Maintainer counts, GitHub stars, or AI confidence scores are not substitutes for these checks.

## AI assistance

AI may help with lexical research, draft comparisons, grammar, and audio preparation, but source quotations and claims must be checked independently. Record meaningful AI use and never publish AI-only translation as Orthodox-reviewed Scripture.
