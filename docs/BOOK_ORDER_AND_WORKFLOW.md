# Approved book order and sequential drafting workflow

**Editorial production decision — 2026-11-12.** This records the approved order in which *Open Orthodox Scripture* will draft all planned books. It is **our own working publishing and translation sequence**, not a claim that the Seventh Ecumenical Council, any single ancient manuscript, or an Orthodox jurisdiction prescribed a universally identical list or order.

**Total production targets: 77 = 49 main Old Testament book workspaces + 1 Old Testament appendix (4 Maccabees) + 27 New Testament books.** The canonical status and editorial placement of some supplementary writings require documented treatment, not silent omission.

## Where this order comes from

We begin with the [49-book Old Testament inventory already established in this project's repository](../translation/ot/README.md), arranged by Law, history, wisdom, minor prophets, and major prophets, and retain its chosen internal order. This grouping is compatible with Eastern Orthodox/Greek Septuagint biblical publishing, but **our precise sequence is an editorial combination, not a transcription of one ancient contents page**.

Historical corroboration and alternatives are preserved in [Ancient book-order alternatives and rationale](ANCIENT_BOOK_ORDER_COMPARISON.md). Key original-era evidence is presented and compared in Henry Barclay Swete's *An Introduction to the Old Testament in Greek* (**1900**, Part II, chapter I, pp. 197–230), particularly his tables for **Codex Vaticanus**, **Codex Alexandrinus**, other manuscripts and patristic lists. The **1900 historical edition** is public domain in the United States; we refer to its bibliographic facts and historical evidence, not a copyrighted modern translation, apparatus, or publisher's unique presentation.

For the **27 New Testament books**, we use the familiar Matthew-to-Revelation order independently attested by the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese's [book listing for the 1904 Patriarchal Greek New Testament](https://onlinechapel.goarch.org/biblegreek/). The listing confirms the order only: it does **not** give us permission to import the website's Greek digital text or establish its rights for this project. Selection of a rights-safe Greek New Testament **base text** remains a separate decision.

## Non-negotiable one-book-at-a-time drafting workflow

1. **Start with Genesis 1.** Verify the precise historical public-domain source edition, its page/verse mapping, and a lawful original transcription pathway; avoid importing unverified electronic texts. Establish the English style and translation conventions needed for the pilot.
2. **Finish chapters in numeric order within the active book** (Genesis 1 through Genesis 50, then Exodus 1 onward). For each chapter: original Greek transcription/source linkage → original English provisional draft → internal independent Greek-to-English audit → variant and theological-interpretation notes → mandatory explanatory footnotes → machine checks.
3. **Complete the entire book to the provisional-draft gate** before starting the next book. The gate requires every intended chapter present, internally checked, cross-referenced to its source; all substantive verified differences linked to book research notes and explanatory footnotes; unresolved issues explicitly listed; no unlicensed outside material; and the book marked **provisional / pending qualified human review**.
4. **Advance only after the book-stage checklist is met.** Open issues are permitted only if visible and non-concealed; do not call a book publication-ready or Church-approved. Cross-book consistency fixes may require later revisiting earlier provisional drafts, with a recorded revision history.
5. **After the entire 77-target provisional corpus is drafted**, begin the qualified external Greek/Orthodox editorial review required by the existing protocol. This rule does not bypass that later review or imply Church endorsement.

**Book status convention:** `not started` → `source ready` → `drafting` → `internally checked` → `provisional book complete / pending qualified human review` → `reviewed / approved` only after later sign-offs. An imported source or an AI-only audit is never ecclesiastical approval.

**Do not silently reorder the books.** Any future order change requires a recorded editorial decision with a dated explanation and updated index/mappings. Readers should be able to see the alternatives considered in [the historical comparison](ANCIENT_BOOK_ORDER_COMPARISON.md).

## Rights and independence

The chosen titles, order, and historical facts are not a license to copy a modern Bible's copyrighted language, notes, typography, translation, or electronic data. Our ordering document is an **independently authored editorial plan**, based on verifiable historical manuscripts, early Church lists, and the project's own Bible book index. Sources and individual translation inputs still pass the project's separate public-domain and provenance checks.
