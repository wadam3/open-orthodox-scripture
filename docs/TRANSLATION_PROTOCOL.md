# Translation protocol — provisional v0.11

**Scope:** Establish a repeatable, documented process for drafting an English Bible translation informed by the Eastern Orthodox tradition. First source tradition: Greek Septuagint; first pilot: **Genesis chapter 1**. The New Testament follows the Orthodox Old Testament in the initial drafting sequence.

## Nonnegotiable boundaries

1. **Free and accessible.** Original translated text will remain freely available, and the intended open license permits commercial reuse with attribution, subject to final legal review.
2. **Independent and not Church-approved.** Do not describe translations as sponsored, authorized, or approved by an Orthodox Church or jurisdiction. Qualified individual reviews are not institutional endorsement.
3. **No outside collaborators during the initial full-Bible drafting phase.** The founder coordinates internal AI-assisted production through completion of a **provisional draft of the entire intended Orthodox canon**. Outside translators, technical maintainers, and language/Orthodox reviewers are invited afterward. Public repository visibility is not a solicitation for immediate contributions.
4. **AI is never an authority.** AI drafts and AI cross-checks are provisional evidence, not independent scholarly or pastoral approval.
5. **Rights and provenance first, with no permission-seeking.** The project will not seek or negotiate licenses or permissions from third parties for source texts. Use independently verified public-domain source material and original project-created content; reject third-party editions, digital transcriptions, notes, translations, or other materials whose copying or redistribution depends on permission, a restrictive license, or uncertain rights. Document provenance and the public-domain basis before import. Our eventual license for *our own original work* is a separate question.
6. **Do not claim approval until it exists.** No chapter is labeled approved until the human linguistic, Orthodox, editorial, and rights gates are complete.

7. **Mandatory explanation of substantive differences.** Whenever a material difference in Greek witnesses, source wording, Orthodox textual usage, book order, verse numbering, or rendering is identified, preserve a separate decision record and attach a clear footnote or explanatory note to the affected passage. Never silently harmonize or invent a difference.

## Approved book order and one-book-at-a-time completion

The [approved 77-target sequence](BOOK_ORDER_AND_WORKFLOW.md) fixes the editorial **drafting order**, starting with Genesis chapter 1 and working through its final chapter before Exodus, continuing sequentially through **49 main Old Testament books**, **4 Maccabees as an appendix**, and **27 New Testament books**. No other main workspaces may be started out of order unless a new dated, recorded editorial decision authorizes the change. Keep every chapter traceable to exact source material, rights verification, original English drafts, internal audits, discrepancy/interpretive notes, and mandatory footnotes.

**Provisional book-complete gate:** each required chapter exists and has passed the agreed internal checks, the source/verse mapping is accounted for, notes and footnotes are linked, and unresolved questions are openly listed. The book may then be marked **provisional — pending qualified external review**, but not **approved, publication-ready, or Church-authorized**. Final qualified language and Orthodox review still occurs **after the complete provisional Bible draft**, under the project's existing sequence.

**History and rights:** the chosen order is our own editorial arrangement, not the exact order of a single ancient manuscript, and not a copied copyrighted modern Bible table of contents. See [ancient book-order alternatives](ANCIENT_BOOK_ORDER_COMPARISON.md), grounded in manuscript comparisons and patristic lists documented in Swete's public-domain 1900 study. Other book groupings, canon distinctions, the Prayer of Manasseh and Odes, Esdras mapping, and alternative forms of Greek Daniel remain visible textual/editorial questions.

## Each book README, each chapter source log, and listening text

**Mandatory sequence:** finish each entire book before the next; within the book, proceed in **chapter-number order**. **One `README.md` in every book folder** must summarize why that exact Greek source was chosen, editions considered and rejected, historical publication and rights evidence, the **actual website name and exact URL(s)** where the edition and page evidence were obtained, manuscript basis and limitations, crosswalks, outstanding controversies, progress, and links to its research notes. Update the book README as decisions change, not merely the central protocol.

**Chapter artifacts:** store the independently produced original English in one numbered structured chapter record (for example, `translation/ot/GEN/001.json`), aligned to stable verse IDs, and a **separate chapter research file** (for example, `notes/ot/GEN/001.md`) documenting source URLs, physical page numbers, verified leaf indices/deep links if known, primary Greek page evidence, word-choice alternatives, internal audit, provenance of any Brenton comparison, and source/discrepancy footnote IDs. No separate README is required for each chapter: the book README is the stable entry point, while chapter notes preserve detailed accountability.

**Exact URL rule:** for **every consulted source** record (a) source title/editor and **exact historical edition/printing**, (b) repository/library or website name, (c) **unmodified source URL**, (d) printed volume/page and verse range, (e) scan leaf index/deep link **only when independently confirmed**, (f) whether it is the actual Greek base, a historical English comparison, catalog/history, or an ancient witness; (g) rights basis and **what was actually accessed/verified**. An index URL is not proof that particular page readings were verified. Do not invent page references, treat OCR as inspected original Greek, or silently change to later editions or modern electronic source texts.

**Audio-first but fidelity-first:** build clean continuous narration from the final chapter text, excluding source metadata, variant IDs and internal research commentary. Provide natural chapter boundaries, pacing/pronunciation guidance and verse-addressable navigation; do not automatically read every verse number or footnote. Preserve substantive reader-facing manuscript notes in ebook/print and accessible companion notes or optional audio segments. Test the Genesis 1 pilot aloud before locking the English register. Research transparency must not degrade audiobook flow.

## Mandatory discrepancy footnotes and neutral source language

**Trigger:** A substantive discrepancy is detected between independently usable ancient witnesses or between our working base and a documented Orthodox textual tradition. This includes meaningful wording differences, omitted or added passages, names, numbering, sequence, canon placement, and Greek version traditions (for example, alternative forms of a biblical book). Pure punctuation or spelling changes without interpretive impact may be logged without a published footnote.

**Workflow for every qualifying passage:**
1. Open a numbered entry in the book's separate notes workspace, with book/chapter/verse, kind of difference, exact independently verifiable public-domain witness references, and date.
2. Describe the readings in original, plain language. Identify what is known, what is uncertain, whether the difference affects meaning, and why a rendering was chosen or left provisional.
3. Attach a concise, neutral footnote or explanation to the relevant verse or book section, with a stable note ID linking to the detailed record. Do not claim that a Church has officially endorsed a particular reading.
4. Run an independent internal check that every flagged substantive discrepancy has its matching reader-facing note. Preserve unresolved entries for later qualified human review.
5. Export corresponding explanations in print footnotes, ebook-linked notes, and accessible notes accompanying audio (spoken selectively when necessary for comprehension).

## Greek-to-English translation choices and Orthodox/Protestant interpretive comparisons

Use the dedicated [Orthodox and Protestant Greek-to-English comparison register](ORTHODOX_PROTESTANT_TRANSLATION_COMPARISONS.md) to track any material wording or interpretive difference encountered during translation. Give each case a stable `GTC-###` ID and cross-link its book-specific research log, relevant source-history file, chapter/verse, and any mandatory published note.

**Required method:** distinguish different underlying Hebrew/Greek textual bases from *alternative English renderings of the same Greek*, and distinguish both from *later theological interpretation*. Document the actual original-language reading and context; viable English alternatives; Greek Orthodox use and other Christian interpretations accurately and without caricature; the project's chosen English rendering and evidence-based reasoning for choosing it; unresolved objections; decision date; and review status. Record **no decision** until the source has been verified.

An Orthodox orientation does **not** authorize changing the Greek text to make it say something it does not say, or rejecting a Protestant-associated English wording merely because of its association. Do not call an alternative a mistranslation without demonstrating a specific linguistic error. Apply existing public-domain and no-permission rules, source-resolution documentation, and reader-footnote requirements. Keep choices provisional until qualified post-draft human review.

## Apostolic Fathers as primary evidence for Orthodox interpretive tradition

For interpretive or translation decisions involving ancient Church tradition, follow [Apostolic Fathers — primary-source evidence and rights policy](APOSTOLIC_FATHERS_SOURCES.md). Prioritize **the authentic ancient writings themselves**, preferably the **original Greek where extant**, as verified in an independently reusable **public-domain historical edition**. If the ancient passage survives in another language or is quoted by a later author, identify that transmission explicitly. Prefer an original project-made English rendering over copying existing translations. **Do not request or negotiate licenses.** A modern freely viewable translation, digital transcription, or commentary is **not** automatically public domain.

For every significant appeal to an Apostolic Father, record the **named early work and section; original-language wording and its verified historical edition; attribution/recension confidence; exact citation and public-domain basis; what the author actually asserts; competing interpretations; and how the evidence supports, limits, or does not determine our English choice**. Link the citation to the relevant book's notes, source register, and, where applicable, the \`GTC-###\` entry in the Orthodox/Protestant comparison document and a reader footnote. Do not convert a Fathers' interpretation into a claim about a Greek biblical manuscript reading without independent textual evidence. Do not attribute the views of one early writer to the entire Eastern Orthodox Church or imply ecclesiastical approval.

A translation choice with significant patristic support remains **provisional** until qualified later review. If no rights-safe primary patristic evidence has been verified, mark the claim **unverified / pending primary source** instead of citing a modern secondary account as if it were ancient testimony.

## Brenton as a public-domain historical English comparator (never the Greek base)

Use the independently verifiable historical English Septuagint translation by **Sir Lancelot Charles Lee Brenton** as a **secondary comparison after our own Greek-to-English draft and internal Greek audit**, under [the historical English translation reference policy](HISTORICAL_ENGLISH_TRANSLATION_REFERENCES.md). The 1844 printing contains an English-only text without the Apocrypha; later nineteenth-century editions differ in coverage. Identify the **specific historical printing and page**. Its U.S. public-domain historical text does not authorize copying modern revised editions, modern editorial notes, or unverified web transcriptions.

Compare the meaning and translation options, **not** by copying, paraphrasing or lightly updating Brenton to make our English. Any material disagreement must be investigated against the project's separately verified public-domain **Greek** witnesses. Document the comparison, accepted or rejected alternatives, and reasons in the book's notes. Link to a `GTC-###` entry and mandatory reader-facing note when the established substantive-difference rules require one. Brenton is **not** an Orthodox Church authority and does not override the Greek text. Keep all decisions provisional until later qualified review.

### Mandatory per-verse Brenton comparison and approval gate — v0.11 (2026-11-12)

**Automatic quality check, never automatic editorial adoption.** In the normal drafting workflow, **after** producing our own original Greek-based English text and doing the separate Greek-to-English semantic audit, automatically perform a **brief Brenton comparison for every verse** in each future chapter where an independently identified, rights-safe historical Brenton edition actually covers the passage. This is a **required routine chapter QA step**; it is **not** the optional, user-requested *Extra Quality Pass*. It does not imply that Genesis 2 has been checked retroactively. No need to request permission merely to run and document the comparison as part of an already authorized chapter-drafting task.

**Minimum per-verse evidence, mandatory in the chapter research log:** book/chapter/verse; status `not_checked`, `compared`, `not_available`, or `blocked`; **precise historical Brenton printing**, printed page and original-scan URL actually consulted; corresponding selected **Swete 1887** Greek page and Greek source locator; a concise independent English-versus-Brenton meaning comparison (agreement, stylistic difference, possible substantive difference, or unresolved); any omission/number/negation/wording alert; outcome/rationale; whether the project English and narration changed; the related note/issue ID; and the review date. Record `not_available` or `blocked` rather than inventing a successful check. Document **all verse entries**, including those with no discrepancy. A chapter-level `brenton_comparison_status = complete` is permitted **only after** every covered verse has a documented `compared` entry and all unavailable/blocked items are explicitly identified; it is a statement about **comparison coverage**, not about verified translation accuracy or publication approval.

**Meaningful differences receive a deeper Greek-first check.** A missing clause, negation, actor, grammatical number, text tradition or theologically important nuance requires targeted comparison with the project's controlling Greek and a stable discrepancy ID, plus a reader-facing note when the existing material-difference rule requires one. **Stylistic divergence alone never triggers a Greek-source confidence penalty or a change to our independent text.**

**Explicit user approval before any Brenton-suggested wording change:** If a proposed modification to our English translation **originates from, is prompted by, or is materially justified by the Brenton comparison**, present the affected verse, exact proposed change, the controlling 1887 Greek evidence, reason, translation/footnote/narration implications, and the status of the concern. **Do not change the English verse, its narration, or adopt a Brenton-motivated substantive textual decision until the user explicitly authorizes that particular proposed change.** Keep it as `proposed_pending_user_approval` if approval is missing; never infer permission from silence, chapter-drafting approval, or approval of a different suggestion. A declined proposal remains documented and does not recur automatically without new evidence. Corrections independently discovered directly from our primary Greek outside the Brenton comparison follow the ordinary mandatory accuracy rules rather than being delayed by this approval gate.

**Do not modify the existing confidence formulas.** Brenton is an **English-quality cross-check and coverage record**, **not an independent Greek manuscript, a calibrated accuracy probability, a source-letter score, or an editorial-bracket score**. It adds **zero points** to `editorial_letter_confidence_percent` and `editorial_confidence_percent`, cannot waive the strict-below-80 flags, and must not produce a blended composite percentage that misrepresents manuscript-source certainty. Log any English-quality concern and its disposition separately.

**Chapter completion gate:** The routine Brenton step must show documented per-verse coverage or transparent `not_available`/`blocked` entries before the comparison is described as complete or the required chapter QA is declared finished. Pending user-approved Brenton-suggested changes must be recorded without applying them; a **material unresolved translation concern** remains a provisional-readiness blocker under the unchanged accuracy safeguards. Rights-safe sources, Greek accuracy, separate source certifications, human/Orthodox review and audio listening approval remain independent requirements. For previous chapters, do **not** backfill `compared` by assumption: Genesis 1 has documented examples but lacks a universal per-verse completion ledger; Genesis 2's comparison is unperformed, and **this amendment does not execute it**.

## Mandatory documentation when discrepancy flags are resolved

**Rule:** Every flagged textual or source-history discrepancy must have a traceable outcome in the **relevant source document**—for example, `docs/SWETE_SOURCE_BACKGROUND.md` for flags about Swete's Greek text. A decision recorded only in a chapter's research log, a Git commit, or an English footnote **is not sufficient**. If no source document exists for the relevant edition or witness, create one within `docs/` and link it from the source workspace before closing the flag. Keep the detailed evidence in the affected book's separate `notes/` workspace; do not replace it with the high-level source record.

**For each flag, follow these steps:**

1. **Keep a stable flag ID** from discovery through resolution. Identify the source edition/witness, affected book and passage(s), what differs or is uncertain, and link the original flag and book-specific research entry.
2. **Investigate with independently verified, rights-safe evidence.** Record exact historical references (edition, volume, page, manuscript witness and verse where possible), relevant Greek readings, alternative interpretations, and any independently supported Orthodox textual-use considerations. Distinguish a demonstrated difference from an unverified report.
3. **Record the outcome in the relevant source document** under that flag ID: the adopted text or editorial treatment, reasons for the decision, alternatives considered, significance for the English translation, and any remaining uncertainty. If the evidence does not justify a choice, explicitly record **unresolved** and the research still needed; never manufacture a solution.
4. **Cross-link the records:** source document ↔ book research log ↔ affected chapter/verse and reader-facing explanatory footnote, where one is required. A source-wide decision must be linked from every affected book or passage. Do not let resolved decisions disappear into commit history.
5. **Record accountability:** decision date, person or team responsible, current state (**open**, **provisional decision**, **verified decision / pending qualified human review**, or **reviewed and approved** only after the applicable formal gates), and the version/commit establishing the decision. An internal or AI-assisted decision is not Orthodox Church approval.
6. **Revisit transparently:** when new evidence changes a decision, update the same flag in the source document with the superseding conclusion, rationale, date, and links; preserve the previous outcome in history rather than silently overwriting its implications.

**Closure gate:** A flag may be marked resolved for the **provisional draft** only after its source document and book log agree, its evidence and rationale are recorded, and any substantive reader-facing footnote/explainer is present or explicitly queued where the corresponding text has not yet been drafted. This does **not** make the passage publication-approved; later qualified human review and release gates still apply. Publication must not omit a required footnote.

**Rights and naming:** Do not copy a restricted edition's apparatus, modern translation, explanatory wording, proprietary content, or protected transcription. Do not mention proprietary publishing products or brands in ordinary reader-facing notes; use generic descriptions such as “another ancient Greek witness” when sufficient. Maintain precise bibliographic references **internally**, using verifiable public-domain manuscripts/editions, because omission of provenance would make a discrepancy impossible to audit. Historical source identification is factual documentation, not approval, sponsorship, or license dependence.

**Evidence gate:** If a supposed difference cannot be substantiated independently from rights-safe material, record it as unverified, do not state it as fact or publish a misleading comparison, and leave the verse flagged for later review. A lack of reusable evidence cannot be fixed by stripping attribution or paraphrasing protected analysis.

## Phase B.1 — Efficient chapter workflow without reducing verification (v0.9 amendment, 2026-11-12)

**Purpose:** Preserve every source, meaning, provenance, discrepancy, confidence, and approval safeguard above while reducing redundant searches, repeated low-impact analysis, and unnecessary GitHub commits. **This amendment changes the order and packaging of work, not any accuracy threshold or release gate.** Use this for Genesis 3 and later chapters, retaining the required one-book-at-a-time, chapter-number order.

### Do once per book; confirm relevant evidence for each chapter

- Create/reuse the book's **source packet** (historical edition, precise printing, public-domain/rights evidence, witness conventions, glossary, source register ID, links, and unresolved tradition/canon questions). **Do not redo the same edition-selection or basic history research for each chapter.**
- For each new chapter, make a **short chapter-specific source map** of the actual printed pages, page breaks and verse ranges, manuscript supplements, and exact URLs. Confirm each image is from the controlling first edition. Consult the scan at the passage level; **a previously verified book identity does not certify any new verse**. Later electronic texts remain diagnostic comparators only.
- Reuse structured JSON, source-log, decision-ID, and narration templates; create stable IDs for any new issue. Keep comprehensive scholarly detail in the chapter log and **link** from the book/source-level records rather than duplicating lengthy prose across every file. The repository should retain all required provenance, not merely a shortened summary.

### Standard chapter production sequence (do not merge distinct checks)

1. **Record timing and inputs.** Log `start_initial_translation` when work actually starts, source/edition ID, source page map, intended verse count, and previously chosen English terminology. Separate hands-on effort from elapsed wall-clock time and interruption time. See [chapter production time log](CHAPTER_PRODUCTION_TIME_LOG.md).
2. **Draft the entire chapter sequentially** from the controlling Greek into a single versioned, original English verse-aligned JSON; mark uncertainty **at discovery** with an item ID and exact source location. Do not copy protected English or fill a doubtful Greek span by assumption. A **complete initial English draft** is a milestone, **not** an accuracy pass; record `first_complete_english_draft`.
3. **Run a genuinely separate Greek-to-English accuracy audit** across every verse, using an explicit verse-by-verse checklist for subjects/referents, negations, omissions/additions, lexical meaning, aspect/number, verse splits, proper names, theological terminology, and existing glossary decisions. Consult the actual controlling source page at material differences. Record every substantive correction and the relevant footnote/decision ID. AI-assisted repeated passes **must be described as AI-assisted**; they do not replace independent qualified human review.
4. **Separate exact-source uncertainty from translation meaning.** Record editorial brackets and Greek letter readings with the **existing [fixed scoring formulas](GREEK_SOURCE_VERIFICATION_PROTOCOL.md)** and item-level evidence, not impressionistic percentages. Enforce **strictly below 80% ⇒ `requires_rigorous_verification_later=true`**; **80% is not flagged**. Recalculate after corrections; preserve source-page links, old readings, reasons, score inputs and change history. Do not artificially raise a score or erase a flag. The same full first-edition photo/diacritic/character and independent review requirements still govern claims of source verification.
5. **Triage verification by consequence, not by convenience.** **Fix immediately** a well-supported original-Greek error or anything that could change translation meaning: omissions, negations, agent/number, substantial variants, proper names, or theologically material readings—even if its heuristic score is high. **Defer for more rigorous later review** low-confidence **non-meaning-changing** bracket edges, exact minor diacritics, and discrepancies with later editions, **only after** logging the evidence and appropriate below-80 flag. Do not repeatedly re-open a passage without new evidence; a deferred issue remains visible and is **not certified**.
6. **Finish the structured audit and reader notes as one coordinated pass.** Verify verse continuity, note links, source provenance, unresolved queue, and intended traditional Orthodox usage without silently amending the Greek. Preserve required explanatory notes for material differences; the uninterrupted audiobook script may link to optional companion notes instead of narrating editorial apparatus.
7. **Generate narration from the current checked English JSON**, never maintain divergent hand-copied text. Run exact script-to-verse equality, structural checks, and any source-confidence validator applicable to **that chapter**. If English changes, regenerate/synchronize narration and recheck the manifest/hash. Actual spoken listening QA remains a **separate** gate and must not be claimed from text equality.
8. **Save GitHub updates at logical milestones** where practical: (a) the initial complete draft and source map, (b) audited corrections with footnotes/Greek uncertainty register, (c) synchronized narration and automated QA results. Multiple files may require separate GitHub commits; **batch the decision and test cycle**, not suppress source-critical corrections or audit history. If a critical error is found, commit it immediately rather than waiting for a batch.
9. **Close as a truthful provisional checkpoint.** Mark `Provisional Draft Complete — Suitable for Trial Reading and Narration` only when all verses exist, a complete semantic audit has been recorded, material errors are resolved or openly identified as blocking, required reader notes are linked, narration equals the checked English, mechanical validators pass, and every unresolved source item remains traceably flagged. Keep schema `status="draft"` and all unearned `transcription_verified`, `rights_reviewed`, and human-review approvals **false**. Do not call this `source-verified`, `publication-ready`, `approved`, or spoken-audio QA complete.

### Unchanged quality gates and stop conditions

- **Never skip:** first-edition identity and per-chapter original page evidence; independent original English drafting; separate complete verse-level semantic audit; source/meaning corrections; mandatory material-difference footnotes; explicit uncertainty/evidence records; reproducible confidence and below-80 flags; narration synchronization; automated structural/content checks; and all later independent language, Orthodox, rights, and audio release checks.
- **Provisional-readiness blocker:** a missing verse, a material unresolved Greek/English discrepancy, absent rights-safe source identification, missing material reader note, failed mechanical QA, or an undocumented consequential flag. Correct before declaring the chapter suitable for trial reading, even if it takes longer.
- **Source/publication approval blocker:** incomplete exact photographic Greek characters, accents, apparatus/brackets, independent source proofreading, rights sign-off, qualified Orthodox/Greek/editorial reviews, or genuine audio-listening QA. A provisional chapter can proceed **without falsely passing** these separate gates; it must remain labelled provisional.
- **No fixed time quota or quality tradeoff.** Spend extra time when a chapter has complicated witnesses or meaningful ambiguities. Historical writing speed is a *planning reference*, not permission to omit a check.

### Measurement and validation

Record `start_initial_translation`, `first_complete_english_draft`, `initial_semantic_audit_finished`, `narration_script_synchronized`, and any `source_verification_started/finished` events **only when actually observed**. Record hours actively worked separately from wall-clock span, count corrected **material** Greek/English errors, verse omissions, open flags and source-image blockers, and link the commits/check results. For Genesis 1, the observed **first partial commit → complete draft** interval was **17m 40s**, while the earlier **~36m kickoff-to-draft** was retrospective; Genesis 2's exact drafting start is **unrecorded**. Neither is a controlled comparison or a promise of future speed.

Pilot this workflow on Genesis 3, then evaluate whether it reduced duplicate source lookups and GitHub iterations **without increasing missed verses, late material corrections, unflagged uncertainty or failed checks**. If the measured error/omission rate worsens or any safeguard is skipped, revise/revert the optimization rather than lower the quality bar. Current specialized Genesis QA scripts cover **chapters 1–2 only**; extend or supplement them for Genesis 3 and future chapters before claiming their automated coverage.

## Phase B.2 — Extra Quality Pass: optional, on request only (v0.10 amendment, 2026-11-12)

An **Extra Quality Pass** is an optional *targeted accuracy-improvement review* of specified provisional chapters. **Never start one automatically, from a timer, after ordinary chapter completion, or solely because confidence flags exist.** It starts only if the user explicitly requests an Extra Quality Pass (or clearly requests an equivalent additional lowest-confidence review) and specifies the scope. The ordinary chapter production workflow and its mandatory safeguards remain unchanged. No Extra Quality Pass is a prerequisite for moving to the next chapter when the normal provisional gates have passed.

### Exact on-request procedure

1. **Honor scope.** Use only the chapter(s) the user explicitly selected; if none can be determined, ask which. Look at the current **letter/verse confidence and editorial-bracket registers together**, not just one type. Default to flagged, unresolved entries below **80%** (strictly less than 80); entries at exactly 80 or above remain eligible only when the user explicitly requests a broader pass. A fully evidence-backed `verification_status="verified"` item is not re-reviewed unless the user asks.
2. **Choose deterministically.** Among open eligible entries, sort by **ascending current confidence percentage**. Ties sort by canonical book ID, numeric chapter, numeric starting verse, and then complete item ID. A blocked entry stays in the queue and cannot be treated as resolved; if no new evidence is accessible, disclose that limitation and seek permission to defer it rather than silently skipping it.
3. **Announce one selected item.** Give its book/chapter/verse, stable ID, **current confidence score**, exact Greek-letter or bracket uncertainty, and link to the controlling original printing. Example at adoption: within Genesis 1, `GEN-001-023-F`, **35%**, is a proposed editorial bracket across a printed line break in verse 23. These are **editorial triage ratings**, not probabilities of translation accuracy.
4. **Investigate only that item.** Examine the controlling historical image, compare the actual Greek letters, accents, breathing/punctuation, printed editorial brackets and relevant apparatus. Independently readable source evidence can justify corrections. Determine whether the issue changes English meaning, footnotes or narration. A later printing or digital comparator remains secondary; do not silently replace the chosen source.
5. **Record the result.** Save what was checked, original and revised Greek if any, primary source reference and page/line, reasoning, previous and **recalculated confidence using the existing fixed scoring formulas**, unchanged/changed strict-below-80 flag, and the review/commit history. If insufficient evidence exists, retain the flag and record an explicit blocker; do not guess or promote the entry to certified. One AI-assisted reading is not an independent two-reviewer verification or publication approval. If English changes, synchronize narration and run existing checks.
6. **Stop and request permission before the next item.** Tell the user what was resolved (or remains blocked), the result and updated score, then identify the **next lowest-confidence candidate by its score, location, item ID, and specific issue**. **Ask whether the user wants that next item reviewed.** Do not start researching or modify the next item until the user explicitly agrees. A request to review one item does not authorize the entire queue; do not interpret silence as permission.
7. **End immediately when declined or stopped.** Preserve the remaining queue and its exact scores for a future explicitly requested Extra Quality Pass. When no unresolved entries remain in the requested scope, report that outcome; do not automatically expand to other chapters or ordinary-score items.

### Quality and separation

The Extra Quality Pass may improve the working translation by focusing effort on the most uncertain source readings, but **it does not replace** ordinary chapter-level semantic audits, source rights review, full diplomatic Greek/diacritic source proofreading, qualified independent language and Orthodox reviews, or genuine audiobook listening checks. Do not use an improved confidence estimate as proof of source accuracy or an institutional endorsement. Keep provisional drafts labelled `draft` until their *separate* release requirements are met. If a serious meaning-changing error is independently discovered through the ordinary workflow, correct it immediately; the optional Extra Quality Pass is **not** a reason to postpone essential corrections.

## Phase C — Complete the provisional Bible before outside collaboration

- Process each Old Testament chapter using the same source, original draft, audit, revision, and provenance steps.
- Keep book boundaries, disputed Septuagint canon/book-order issues, and alternate readings explicitly documented. Resolve policy questions by documented decision rather than silent model output.
- After the defined Old Testament inventory is drafted, select and rights-check an appropriate Greek New Testament edition and repeat the process for each NT chapter.
- Track progress as **not started / draft / internally checked / unresolved / ready for human review**. These are project-progress labels, not theological approvals.
- Freeze and tag a reproducible **complete provisional draft** only when every planned chapter is accounted for; publish the manifest and the list of unresolved issues. The provisional milestone does **not** authorize a reviewed print or audio Bible.

## Phase D — Qualified outside review, after the complete provisional draft

- Invite independent Greek-language editors, Orthodox clergy/scholars where appropriate, English editors, and technical maintainers.
- Record reviewer qualifications and consent to public credit; do not disclose private identities without permission.
- Verify source fidelity, theology and liturgical terminology, disputed readings, readability, attribution, canon/order, and internal consistency.
- Require explicit rights sign-off, language-editor sign-off, a designated qualified Orthodox-reviewer sign-off, and release-editor confirmation **for each approved chapter**. A GitHub merge or AI consensus is not sign-off.
- Maintain open corrections, editorial decisions, revision history, and releases; do not imply reviewer participation is Church-wide approval.

## Phase E — Publications

- After the appropriate human review gates, export a fixed and reproducible text release into free ebooks/digital files, print-ready assets, and narrated audiobooks.
- Use the same approved text/version in all channels. Preserve attribution, independence disclaimer, and all mandatory discrepancy explanations in the format appropriate to print, ebook, and accessible audio.
- Produce narrated versions from approved text; prototype audio internally if useful but never mislabel it as reviewed Scripture.
- Follow \`docs/PUBLISHING_DISTRIBUTION.md\` for paid Audible, free YouTube, possible Spotify, print formats, and publisher restrictions.

## Chapter record minimum

Each draft should include: book ID, book title, chapter number, language, status, precise source edition and reference, rights status, ordered verses, stable discrepancy-footnote IDs and separate evidence/log entries, translation notes/variants, terminology decisions, AI-assistance disclosure, version or revision, outstanding review questions, and eventual named human sign-offs.
