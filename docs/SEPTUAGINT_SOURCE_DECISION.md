## Working base for Genesis 1

**Select for the first internal drafting pilot:** Henry Barclay Swete (ed.), *The Old Testament in Greek According to the Septuagint*, **volume I, first edition (Cambridge University Press, 1887)**, Genesis. The precise base is the historical 1887 printing, **not** a modern reprint, licensed machine-readable transcription, or a claim of official Church approval.

- Primary historical witness: https://archive.org/details/oldtestamentingr01swet (Wellesley College Library scan; item oldtestamentingr01swet).
- Catalog and rights statement: https://commons.ptsem.edu/id/oldtestamentingr01swet (Princeton Theological Commons: 1887, volume 1, "No Known Copyright").
- Editorial history: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/old-testament-in-greek-according-to-the-septuagint/58D0D0A49BA2F1D53DB004EA01A1C052 (Cambridge describes Swete's use of Codex Vaticanus and supplements from Alexandrinus).

## Rights assessment and strict source handling

- The historical 1887 volume is **public domain in the United States** based on publication date; the Internet Archive record also labels it NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT. This supports original translation work from the **1887 printed Greek source**, with transcription checked against page images. This is source-reuse research, not an opinion from legal counsel.
- **Do not import modern third-party digital Greek datasets that depend on license terms, permission, or uncertain rights.** We will not request or negotiate permissions for source texts. Use the independently verified public-domain historical edition, with original transcription or another demonstrably public-domain pathway.
- Do not treat OCR as a verified Greek text; check diacritics, word forms, verse boundaries, and variants against original pages. No copyrighted modern critical apparatus, notes, or translations may be copied merely because the ancient material is public domain.
- If another transcription is proposed, document its exact origin and affirmative public-domain basis first; do not import it if using or redistributing it requires a third-party license or permission.

## Substantive differences

When independently verifiable ancient witnesses or Orthodox textual usage differ materially from the working base, open a Genesis research-log entry and create a linked, neutrally worded footnote/explainer for the relevant passage. ## Orthodox textual context

Swete is a scholarly Septuagint edition based chiefly on Codex Vaticanus. It is **not an ecclesiastically authorized Orthodox Bible**. The Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America and Hellenic Bible Society have a separate initiative to present and refine the living Septuagint text used in Orthodox worship (https://septuagint.bible/). Consult Orthodox liturgical readings and permitted comparisons at later review stages, noting material differences without reproducing unlicensed editorial material.

The precise Orthodox Old Testament canon/book inventory, verse-number mapping, translation style, and review standards remain separate editorial decisions. A public-domain source does not itself authenticate an English translation.
