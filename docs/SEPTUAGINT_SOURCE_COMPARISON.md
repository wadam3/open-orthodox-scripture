# Greek Septuagint source editions considered — provenance, Orthodox use, and reuse eligibility

**Research and source-selection record — 2026-11-12. Decision: Swete's 1887 first edition, Volume I, remains the *provisional* Genesis 1 drafting base; no Greek text has been imported or verified verse by verse.** This is a comparison of the historical *Greek* source editions discussed for Open Orthodox Scripture. It is not a comparison of modern English Bible translations and does not imply approval by any Orthodox Church.

**Why Brenton is not listed as a fifth Greek edition:** Sir Lancelot Charles Lee Brenton's nineteenth-century **English** Septuagint is documented separately as a [public-domain historical English comparison reference](HISTORICAL_ENGLISH_TRANSLATION_REFERENCES.md). It may be consulted **after** independent drafting from Greek, but is not our Greek source text.

The project requires independently verified **public-domain Greek source material** and originally prepared English translations. It will **not seek or negotiate any third-party source licenses or permissions**. We must distinguish (a) ancient Greek biblical writings, (b) a manuscript such as Vaticanus, (c) a scholar's historical printed editorial text, and (d) a modern edition, critical apparatus, digital transcription, or website. Their provenance and reuse status are not interchangeable.

**Important limitation:** The 1887 public-domain conclusion is for Swete's **historical printed volume in the United States**, not automatically its modern scanned images, OCR, transcriptions, publisher reproductions, supplementary annotations, or distribution outside the United States. A modern edition is not necessarily "proprietary" just because it is modern, but **we do not import it unless an independently verified public-domain basis exists**, consistent with the stricter project rule.

## 1. Swete: reasons for the provisional selection

- **Publication origin:** Henry Barclay Swete edited his three-volume Septuagint at Cambridge: Volume I in 1887 (Genesis through 4 Kingdoms); II in 1891; III in 1894.
- **Greek-text origin:** He generally followed the fourth-century Vaticanus manuscript, with supplementary readings chiefly from the fifth-century Alexandrinus and other witnesses when necessary. This is a **manuscript-oriented edition**, not the original third-century BCE translation and not one infallible manuscript.
- **Genesis-specific caution:** The surviving original Vaticanus lacks its opening Genesis text, so **Genesis 1 is not based on surviving Vaticanus leaves**. Before transcription, record Swete's precise 1887 printed pages and manuscript markers. See [SWETE-FLAG-002](SWETE_SOURCE_BACKGROUND.md).
- **Why selected:** Complete historical Greek base, traceable editorial method, public-domain U.S. printed first edition, and substantial Old Testament coverage without copying an existing English translation.
- **Why provisional:** Swete's readings cannot be assumed to be the earliest recoverable Greek or identical to Orthodox worship. Later manuscripts and scholarly readings may disagree, and an actual Greek Genesis 1 source file still must be created and checked.
- **Status:** **working source selected; no imported Greek text, no finalized English rendering, no Church approval**.

See [Genesis source decision](SEPTUAGINT_SOURCE_DECISION.md), [Swete source history and flags](SWETE_SOURCE_BACKGROUND.md), and [Genesis source-intake checklist](../sources/ot/greek/GEN/README.md).

## 2. Rahlfs and Rahlfs–Hanhart: why not our copyable base

Alfred Rahlfs published *Septuaginta* in **1935**; Robert Hanhart revised it in **2006**. Unlike Swete's generally manuscript-based running text, this is an **edited critical Greek text** with readings selected from several manuscripts. The later revision made extensive corrections and expansions. It is standard scholarly infrastructure and informs the history of Greek Orthodox scholarly and publishing choices; however, scholarly quality does not grant unrestricted republication rights.

**Disposition:** Do **not** ingest any modern Rahlfs or Rahlfs–Hanhart text, critical apparatus, punctuation, metadata compilation, or third-party digital transcription unless independent public-domain reuse is positively documented (not presently established). The mere possibility of a different reading is a **research flag**, not a verified discrepancy, until supported by rights-safe independent evidence. No permission requests.

## 3. Göttingen: why it is valuable but not our base

The Göttingen *Septuaginta: Vetus Testamentum Graecum* is a book-by-book critical edition series begun in the twentieth century, with **Genesis edited by John William Wevers in 1974**. It weighs extensive manuscript and early-version evidence, aiming at earlier recoverable Greek forms. Its method and detailed textual apparatus differ from a historical diplomatic/manuscript-oriented edition.

**Disposition:** Important **bibliographic and methodological comparator**, not a copyable text or apparatus for an openly redistributable corpus on our current rights evidence. Even if a scholarly argument alerts us to a variant, we must independently verify it from sources meeting our project rules before asserting it in the translation or reader notes. No permissions or negotiated licenses.

## 4. Church of Greece tradition: relevant ecclesiastically, but no copying rights inferred

**Apostoliki Diakonia**, the Church of Greece's publishing/missionary organization, has issued Greek Septuagint publications, including a **1980 edition based on Rahlfs's critical work** and a **1997 printing with stronger attention to the ecclesiastical tradition**. The relationship is therefore historically more specific than simply saying "the Church of Greece text equals Rahlfs." Separate modern printings and web presentations must not be assumed identical.

The Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America and Hellenic Bible Society maintain a Septuagint initiative intended to make Orthodox liturgical and devotional texts available and to prepare a carefully edited text. That ecclesiastical connection is significant for **assessing Orthodox usage**, but **does not establish public-domain status or an unrestricted right to redistribute the published Greek text**. Nor does any Church of Greece publication by itself imply that all Orthodox churches prescribe precisely the same modern critical edition.

**Disposition:** Identify questions about Orthodox liturgical readings and compare through **independently lawful public-domain primary witnesses** or original project research; document unresolved liturgical-use differences without copying protected source material or unverified contemporary commentaries. Do not seek permission.

## 5. How discrepancies between editions must be handled

1. For the active book/chapter, lock a precise rights-reviewed historical source edition, printed page reference, and independent Greek transcription process. We begin with Swete **1887 Volume I**, Genesis 1.
2. When a candidate difference arises, **classify** it: (i) differing ancient Greek manuscript readings, (ii) modern editorial reconstruction, (iii) Orthodox ecclesiastical/liturgical textual usage, or (iv) differences of book/verse division. Do not conflate a publisher's editorial choice with an original ancient manuscript witness.
3. **Verify any substantive difference independently** using public-domain historical printed text/manuscript evidence. If we cannot independently substantiate it without restricted modern content, log an **unverified candidate** rather than asserting or copying it.
4. In the relevant `notes/ot/<BOOK_ID>/` research log, record exact source manuscript/edition references and original-language evidence; cross-link Swete flags and the [Orthodox–Protestant translation comparison register](ORTHODOX_PROTESTANT_TRANSLATION_COMPARISONS.md) when applicable.
5. **Explain verified substantive differences** in an original, neutral reader-facing footnote/explainer, including a linked audio-accessible note. State why our English version adopts a particular Greek reading. Avoid unsupported claims of Church approval or "correctness."
6. When a discrepancy is resolved for the provisional draft, **update the relevant source-history document** with the final/provisional choice, alternatives, reasons, date, evidence, link to the book's log and footnote, and remaining review status. The [Translation Protocol](TRANSLATION_PROTOCOL.md) governs the closure gate.

## 6. Rights and source-register status

- **No third-party source permissions or licenses.** For all material we actually copy/import, document exact provenance, *affirmative* public-domain basis, scan/page-to-text mapping, verification date, and checksum in the designated source workspace and the [canonical source register](SOURCE_REGISTER.csv).
- **The source register currently contains only its header**. This comparison identifies *candidate editions*, not imported records; do not mark these modern editions approved for reuse. It does not substitute for source-level intake.
- Names of historical people, manuscripts, and editions are necessary **internally for reproducible attribution**; ordinary reader-facing footnotes should use plain descriptions rather than gratuitous protected brand/publisher names.
- This is an independently authored scholarly comparison of publication facts and source methods, **not a copied modern editorial apparatus**. Other-jurisdiction rights and the downstream publishing terms require review before distribution.
