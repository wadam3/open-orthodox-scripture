# Apostolic Fathers — primary-source evidence and rights policy

**Status: source-selection and interpretive research guide, v0.1 (2026-11-12).** No Apostolic Fathers text has been imported, verified passage by passage, or endorsed as an interpretation of any particular verse. This policy complements the [Translation Protocol](TRANSLATION_PROTOCOL.md) and [Orthodox/Protestant translation comparisons](ORTHODOX_PROTESTANT_TRANSLATION_COMPARISONS.md).

## Purpose

When the project explains a Greek-to-English translation decision, ancient Christian reception, apostolic tradition, or an Orthodox interpretive position, **prioritize direct evidence from the actual historical writings of early Christian authors**, especially the Apostolic Fathers, over modern retellings or copyrighted commentaries. Choose verified public-domain historical editions or new project-created transcriptions/translations; **do not seek licenses or permissions from third parties**.

"Apostolic Fathers" is a modern designation for a group of early Christian works, not a claim that every author was an apostle, that every attribution is certain, or that every document has equal authority in the Eastern Orthodox Church. Patristic texts are evidence about early interpretation and practice, **not a replacement for Scripture or a means to alter the Greek wording of Scripture**.

## Evidence priority for each claim

1. **Biblical text first.** Establish the project’s actual documented Greek biblical source and sound grammatical possibilities. A patristic interpretation may illuminate how it was understood but cannot change what the manuscript reads.
2. **Primary patristic wording second.** Prefer the ancient **Greek wording of the Father’s work where it survives**. For material transmitted only in Latin, Syriac, Armenian, quotation fragments, or other versions, identify the language and state that the original Greek is absent or uncertain where applicable.
3. **Historically documented edition.** For each patristic passage, record exact work, author/attribution, chapter/section, recension or manuscript witnesses where known, edition, editor, original publication year, volume/page or stable section, and the scan used to verify the text. Original project translations from the historic language are preferable to copying later English translations.
4. **Reception and attribution caution.** Distinguish what an early writer expressly says from modern inferences; distinguish a text’s composition date from a later manuscript’s age. Flag disputed authorship, recensions, fragments preserved by later writers, and possible interpolations rather than representing uncertain material as an unquestioned Apostolic Father quotation.
5. **Multiple perspectives.** Where material disagreements exist, describe the relevant early witnesses fairly, including divergent readings and uncertainties. Do not manufacture an early Orthodox/Protestant dispute: the Protestant Reformation happened much later. A Father is evidence of *early Christian reception*, not proof that all present-day Orthodox or Protestants adopt a single interpretation.
6. **Decision documentation.** Log each significant use of patristic evidence in the appropriate book-specific research file and (when relevant) a stable **GTC-###** comparison record. Explain exactly how the passage influenced or **did not determine** an English rendering. Cross-link source-history discrepancies under their separate flag IDs. Put an original reader-facing note where the protocol calls for one.

## Strict intellectual-property and verification gate

- The underlying ancient writings are out of copyright by age, but **modern edited texts, translations, apparatus, introductions, photographs, and electronic databases can have separate protected contributions or conditions of use**.
- For this project, accept only **independently verified public-domain historical editions and project-created original work** for material we copy, import, or redistribute. Openly accessible is **not** the same as public domain; a freely viewable website, "open source" label, or a permissive-license offer does not automatically qualify under the project’s no-third-party-license rule.
- **Do not import** any text from a website or modern digital transcription until we establish the precise underlying edition, U.S. public-domain basis, usable provenance path, transcription method, and relevant geographic rights concerns. If status is unclear, use the historical printed page images to prepare an independently verified original transcription, or leave the item on hold. No permission requests.
- Before import, document the rights basis, stable URI, verification date, and any constraints in the **single canonical** [source register](SOURCE_REGISTER.csv). Do not treat the following candidate as "approved for reuse" until its exact pages and intake pathway are checked.
- Never copy modern copyrighted explanatory wording even when paraphrasing a public-domain ancient passage. In internal research records, accurately identify historical sources by name and bibliographic details. Reader-facing notes can use clear, generic descriptions, but should not conceal evidence needed to check a claim.
- Source verification or an internal AI analysis is **not** independent qualified Greek, historical, or Orthodox theological review. Final approval still follows the project’s later human-review process.

## Initial historical edition to evaluate — candidate only

**Joseph Barber Lightfoot and J. R. Harmer (ed.), *The Apostolic Fathers*, revised texts, London: Macmillan, 1891.**

- Historical print edition catalog: https://openlibrary.org/books/OL7245419M/The_Apostolic_fathers
- Historical scan identifier: https://archive.org/details/apostolicfathers00lighuoft
- The **1891 publication is public domain in the United States by publication date**, but the exact printed Greek passages, electronic extraction/transcription route, and status for use in other jurisdictions must be checked independently before import.
- The edition covers writings historically grouped with the Apostolic Fathers, including First Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, the Didache, the Epistle of Barnabas, the Shepherd of Hermas, the Epistle to Diognetus, and associated texts/fragments. **Inclusion in an edition is not proof of traditional attribution or Orthodox canonical status.**
- Do **not** silently substitute any modern revision or commentary for the actual 1891 printing.

This is an **edition candidate**, not a claim to possess autograph manuscripts. Its value is access to ancient writings through a historically traceable printed text. The writings themselves may have complex manuscript transmission.

## Priority writings to examine (all require passage-level verification)

| Early work | Relevance to potential translation decisions | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First Clement**, especially chapters 42–44 | Church order, ministry, succession, appointment language | Verify the actual passage and whether the proposed reading follows its wording |
| **Ignatius of Antioch**, especially *Smyrnaeans* 8 and *Philadelphians* 4 | Bishop, presbyters, Eucharist, church unity | Specify the authentic middle recension, not an unexamined later extended version |
| **Polycarp, To the Philippians** | Moral exhortation, community and ministry terminology | Distinguish authorial text from later reports about Polycarp |
| **Didache**, especially chapters 7, 9–10, 14–15 | Baptism, Eucharist, gathered worship, ministers | Anonymous early church-order text; do not describe it as literally authored by the Twelve |
| **Martyrdom of Polycarp** | Early Christian testimony and interpretive reception | Different genre and transmission; verify authorship, dating, and later additions before strong claims |
| **Barnabas, Hermas, Diognetus, and fragments attributed to Papias** | Additional early Christian interpretive evidence | Some anonymous, pseudonymous, fragmentary, or preserved through later writers; record attribution and transmission uncertainty explicitly |

These are **research leads**, not ready-made proof-texts or definitive Orthodox translations.

## Required record fields for a patristic citation

- Scripture passage ID; the question under review; linked source variant or GTC comparison ID if relevant.
- Author as historically attributed, specific work, section, language, recension, and textual authenticity confidence.
- Verbatim original-language excerpt **only if verified from the accepted public-domain historical witness**, or an original project translation with a checked source; no modern copyrighted excerpts.
- Exact historical edition, print page / section and scan URI; rights verification and source-register ID.
- Original plain-language summary of what the passage **actually** states, what it does not state, and how it might inform an Orthodox reading.
- Alternative explanations, applicability to the biblical Greek, chosen translation impact (if any), unresolved uncertainty, and status.
- Reviewer/date and cross-links to source documentation, book notes, and any necessary reader-facing note.
