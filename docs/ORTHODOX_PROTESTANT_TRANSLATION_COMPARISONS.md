# Orthodox and Protestant Greek-to-English translation and interpretation comparisons

**Status: Working research register, v0.1 (2026-11-12). No final translation decisions recorded.** This document records places where Eastern Orthodox and Protestant readers, translators, or textual traditions may differ in how Scripture is rendered or understood. It does **not** designate any denomination's reading as a mistranslation merely because it differs from another tradition. Neither "Orthodox" nor "Protestant" denotes one uniform set of English Bible renderings or theological opinions.

**Project purpose:** Record *what the Greek actually allows*, how independently verifiable source traditions compare, what alternative English renderings mean, and **why our project eventually adopts a particular rendering**. Our original English translation should be faithful to its identified Greek source and attentive to the Eastern Orthodox, especially Greek Orthodox, scriptural and liturgical tradition. It should not be engineered to disagree with Protestant translations simply for being Protestant.

The initial full-Bible draft remains provisional and independently produced; no entry implies Orthodox Church approval, a qualified human review, or permission to copy modern translations.

## Distinguish three kinds of differences

1. **Different textual base / witnesses:** Two English Bibles may translate different source texts (for example, Greek Septuagint versus Hebrew Old Testament). First identify and verify the underlying textual difference. This is *not* necessarily a competing English translation of the same Greek words. Resolve associated source-document flags under the [Translation Protocol](TRANSLATION_PROTOCOL.md).
2. **Different defensible English renderings of the same Greek:** Greek syntax, word range, idiom, or historical context may allow multiple English expressions. Show the genuine options, not imaginary alternatives or a theological interpretation presented as a dictionary definition.
3. **Different theological or ecclesiastical interpretation:** The same correctly translated Greek may be interpreted differently by Christian traditions. Document the interpretive difference separately rather than asserting that the competing translation is linguistically wrong.

Some passages involve more than one kind. Record the kind(s) precisely and never infer the religious identity of an English translator from one wording.

## Required decision record for each material comparison

Create one stable **GTC-###** record here, plus a detailed book-specific record under `notes/ot/<BOOK_ID>/` or the future New Testament notes workspace. Required fields:

- **Passage and cross-references:** canonical book/chapter/verse, Greek and alternate numbering if applicable, and source-text edition/version and page or manuscript identifiers.
- **Category:** textual-base difference, Greek-to-English lexical/syntactic ambiguity, interpretive/theological difference, or a combination. Explain which is supported and which is only suspected.
- **Original-language evidence:** exact verified public-domain Greek reading(s), morphology and syntax, literary setting; Hebrew or other witnesses only where germane and independently verifiable.
- **Possible original English renderings:** each reasonable option, grammatical evidence for and against it, and any nuance or ambiguity lost. Do not copy or adapt modern copyrighted Bible translation wording or proprietary explanatory notes.
- **Historical reception and competing explanations:** describe evidence-based Greek Orthodox liturgical/patristic interpretation and accurately represented Protestant interpretation(s), distinguishing the groups and their variety where known. A doctrinal view is not proof of Greek grammar.
- **Project decision and rationale:** chosen Greek base, final or provisional **English wording**, reasons for choosing it rather than alternatives, meaning-level implications, and what it does **not** settle. If evidence is incomplete, enter **no decision — open**.
- **Evidence, rights, and accountability:** independently verifiable references, rights assessment as needed, date, contributor or review owner, uncertainty, status, commit or version, and link to the book's research log.
- **Reader-facing explanation:** original neutral-language footnote/explainer, or an explicit note that the passage is not yet drafted. Mark substantive differences for mandatory publication notes; merely different theological interpretations do not automatically prove an alternate Greek reading or linguistic error.

**Decision states:** `candidate — needs Greek verification` → `researching` → `provisional choice` → `internally checked / pending qualified human review` → `reviewed and approved` **only after the project's established gates**. Never bypass later qualified human review.

**Cross-references:** If a comparison also involves a source edition's disputed reading, update its matching source-history document (such as [Swete source background](SWETE_SOURCE_BACKGROUND.md)) under the original source flag ID. A decision in this register does not substitute for the source-document and book-log resolution rule. The translation text and required verse note must link back to the same GTC record.

## Mandatory early-Church primary-source check for tradition-based arguments

When a proposed English rendering is justified by **apostolic tradition, patristic interpretation, or early Greek Orthodox reception**, seek direct testimony from verified **public-domain historical editions of the Apostolic Fathers**, using the [Apostolic Fathers source policy](APOSTOLIC_FATHERS_SOURCES.md). For each applicable \`GTC-###\` case record the precise ancient author/work/section, textual recension or disputed attribution, original language (Greek where available), verified historical edition and rights basis, a fresh project-created English explanation, and the specific bearing (or lack of bearing) on Greek-to-English wording.

**Distinguish** (a) Greek textual evidence, (b) earliest Christian interpretation, and (c) later doctrinal explanations. An Apostolic Father is not automatically conclusive for the original sense of a biblical Greek word; alternative renderings still require grammatical analysis. Do not cite copyrighted modern commentaries or assume that every "Apostolic Fathers" attribution is authentic. If no admissible historical source is verified, keep the comparison open rather than inventing patristic support. Link detailed primary-source citations to the book's research notes and canonical source register. The historical writers predate the Protestant Reformation and should not be labeled "Orthodox versus Protestant" witnesses in their own time.

**Initial patristic research leads:** \`GTC-004\` (2 Thessalonians 2:15) may benefit from carefully qualified study of First Clement and other genuine early testimony to tradition; \`GTC-005\` (Acts 20:28) may benefit from First Clement 42–44 and the genuine Ignatian letters on ministry. These are **leads, not verified interpretations or chosen English renderings**.

### GTC-001 — Isaiah 7:14 and Matthew 1:23

- **Observed issue to verify:** The Septuagint's Greek `παρθένος` (*parthenos*) is commonly understood as "virgin," while the Hebrew `עלמה` (*almah*) has a different lexical range often expressed "young woman." Matthew 1:23 uses the Greek tradition.
- **Key distinction:** This is primarily a **Greek-versus-Hebrew source-language comparison**; it would be misleading to say both English alternatives necessarily translate the *same Greek* equally.
- **Translation question:** Confirm Swete's precise Greek Isaiah text. Once verified, what is the most faithful English form and what reader note is appropriate about the Hebrew difference and New Testament use?
- **Provisional project decision:** **None.** Do not replace Greek with Hebrew or assume all Protestant editions make the same choice.
- **Detailed notes destination:** `notes/ot/ISA/`; link Matthew when the New Testament workspace exists.

### GTC-002 — Psalm 39:7 in the Greek tradition; Psalm 40:6–7 in other numbering

- **Observed issue to verify:** The Greek Psalter tradition contains the idea of a **body prepared**, cited in Hebrews 10:5, while the Hebrew tradition speaks of **ears** prepared/opened/dug. The exact verse designation varies between printed Psalters.
- **Key distinction:** Separate the **source-text divergence**, the **translation of each source**, and the New Testament author's use of the Greek form. Do not silently mix Greek and Hebrew wordings.
- **Translation question:** Verify the edition's Greek reading and verse mapping in Swete, and whether manuscript variants alter this comparison.
- **Provisional project decision:** **None.**
- **Detailed notes destination:** `notes/ot/PSA/`.

### GTC-003 — Romans 5:12

- **Observed issue to verify:** The Greek `ἐφ’ ᾧ πάντες ἥμαρτον` (*eph' hō pantes hēmarton*) has generated discussions about whether the English should express a causal relation ("because all sinned"), refer back to death ("on account of which all sinned"), or be understood through an older "in whom" interpretive tradition. These are **not necessarily equally well-supported grammatical translations**.
- **Key distinction:** A historically influential Western Latin interpretation is not the same thing as a unified Protestant translation; Orthodox scholars also disagree over particular arguments. Evaluate the Greek grammar before drawing doctrinal conclusions about ancestral sin, inherited guilt, and death.
- **Translation question:** Identify a verified public-domain Greek New Testament base and compare grammatical, contextual, and patristic evidence before deciding.
- **Provisional project decision:** **None; New Testament source edition not yet selected.**

### GTC-004 — 2 Thessalonians 2:15

- **Observed issue to verify:** `παραδόσεις` (*paradoseis*) can be explained as "traditions" or "teachings handed down." The immediate context refers to instruction conveyed orally and by letter.
- **Key distinction:** The interpretation of **which apostolic teachings remain authoritative and how they are transmitted** is distinct from what the Greek noun itself says. Some Protestant communities recognize authoritative apostolic tradition but disagree about its preservation and later ecclesiastical authority.
- **Translation question:** Which English rendering preserves Paul's transmission language without inserting a later doctrinal conclusion into the translation?
- **Provisional project decision:** **None; New Testament source edition not yet selected.**

### GTC-005 — Acts 20:28

- **Observed issue to verify:** `ἐπισκόπους` (*episkopous*) may be rendered "overseers" or "bishops" in English. The latter may suggest a more specifically developed church office to modern readers.
- **Key distinction:** Assess word sense and first-century ecclesiastical context before deciding whether an established Orthodox term or a broader functional term communicates the Greek best. Do not treat "bishop" as automatically an error or "overseer" as automatically theologically neutral.
- **Translation question:** Which English form is faithful to Luke's immediate context, and would a note preserve the Orthodox historical understanding more clearly than a loaded translation choice?
- **Provisional project decision:** **None; New Testament source edition not yet selected.**

## How to work through this register

1. When a passage is reached during chapter drafting, check whether a candidate entry exists; add one when a material denominationally significant linguistic or interpretive difference is discovered.
2. Verify the source text from historical public-domain evidence and classify the type of disagreement correctly. Preserve precise scholarly provenance internally without copying restricted editions or licensed electronic texts.
3. Compare **multiple original English possibilities** and articulate their strengths and limitations. Document Orthodox usage and fairly describe other traditions only when there is verifiable support.
4. Record the decision and why it was chosen, or leave the entry explicitly open. Keep book notes, the relevant source-history document, and a substantive reader footnote cross-linked under the Translation Protocol.
5. Revisit provisional decisions during qualified post-draft review. Maintain prior decisions and reasons in history if a conclusion changes.

**Publication principle:** Reader-facing footnotes should explain the actual text, context, and meaningful alternatives in neutral language. They need not categorize people by denomination or name proprietary Bible products. Do not publish conjecture or generic claims that "Protestants mistranslate" a passage without demonstrable evidence. Our publication must remain honest about uncertainty and independent of third-party permissions.
